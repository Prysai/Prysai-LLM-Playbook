# Six messages for a bounded research check

Use this loop only with public or authorized material you can inspect. It helps
you keep a small research question and its evidence separate. It does not
browse, prove a source true, establish current facts, or make a high-stakes
decision. Stop if the task requires secrets, unlicensed material, a private
person's data, or permission to publish, buy, hire, diagnose, or act.

## 1. Freeze one decision
```text
I need to make this decision: [decision]. Help me turn it into one answerable
question. State the decision owner, date boundary, what would change the
decision, and what is out of scope. Do not search, recommend, rank, or invent
facts. Ask one question only if a decision-critical detail is missing.
```
## 2. Name claims and source owners
```text
For the bounded question, list up to three material claims and the source type
that could own each one: official product page, primary research, law or
policy, first-party dataset, or named institutional record. Say what each type
cannot prove. Do not invent URLs, titles, quotes, citations, or a conclusion.
```
## 3. Read supplied material, not imagined sources
```text
Using only the public or authorized excerpts and links I provide, make a claim
ledger with: claim | direct support | interpretation | conflict or gap | access
date | next check. Mark anything not present as unknown. Do not say a link was
opened unless its content is in this conversation.
```
## 4. Test one sentence
```text
Check whether this exact sentence is supported by the supplied material:
[sentence]. Separate direct support, inference, conflict, and unknowns. Quote
only supplied text. If support is missing, help me narrow the sentence instead
of filling the gap.
```
## 5. Look for a decision-changing exception
```text
For the current claim ledger, name one plausible newer revision, exception, or
conflicting source owner that could change the decision. Say why it matters and
what minimal permitted check remains. Do not claim the research is complete.
```
## 6. End with a stop receipt
```text
Return a research receipt: decision | question | scope | supplied materials |
direct support | inference | conflicts | unknowns | access dates | next check |
stop reason. End with exactly one status: research_plan, scope_checked_for_supplied_list, blocked, or not_run. Do not call the answer exhaustive, current, or ready for a high-stakes decision.
```

The receipt is a bounded record, not proof that an LLM researched correctly or
that a conclusion should be acted on.
