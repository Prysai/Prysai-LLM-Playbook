# What an LLM is, and what it is not

**Unit:** `core-llm-boundaries`
**Status:** `candidate`. **Run status:** `not_run`.
**Time:** about 15 minutes. **Prerequisite:** none. You do not need Codex,
Git, a paid account, or a tool.

## The result you should keep

By the end, write an explanation card in your own words and make two boundary
decisions. A successful card should let another person see:

- how a text LLM uses the context supplied to a request;
- why fluent text is not, by itself, evidence of a true claim; and
- which parts belong to a model, a chat product, or an external tool.

This is a bounded explanation task, not a test of everything a model can do.

## Start with one working sentence

A modern text LLM is a model trained to estimate and generate sequences of
tokens. Many autoregressive models generate by predicting a next token from the
available context, one step at a time. Additional training and product layers
shape how a deployed system responds.

That sentence is a useful working model, not a complete definition of every
language, multimodal, or deployed system called an LLM. It explains why a
system can continue text, translate, summarize, or draft without implying that
every output is true or that every product has the same capabilities.

### Three boundaries to keep visible

1. **Model:** learned parameters generate a response from an input context.
   A base model generates text; it is not automatically a verified database,
   a person, or an actor with permission.
2. **Product:** a chat interface may add system instructions, retrieval,
   memory, files, moderation, or **search, retrieval, files, memory, or
   tools**. Those layers can change what the user observes and what data is
   available.
3. **Tool or Agent:** a connected tool may calculate, read a file, or send a
   request when an interface authorizes it. A proposed call, a button, or an
   Agent summary is not evidence that an external action happened.

Capability, authorization, and evidence are separate questions. Ask them in
that order before a conversation can become an action.

## A five-minute boundary check

This is a text-only observation. Use a chat surface if you have one, but do not
enable search, upload a file, or provide private information.

First, complete this sentence without asking a model:

> The club meets Tuesday at 18:00, and the room number is ...

Write down two possible continuations and mark which one is supported by the
sentence. The correct answer is that neither room number is supplied. Your
brain can make a plausible completion too; plausibility and evidence are
different things.

Now send only this fictional notice and request:

```text
Notice: "The club meets Tuesday at 18:00. Bring a notebook. The room number
will be confirmed later."

Task: rewrite the notice for a new member in two sentences. Keep every stated
fact. Put missing details in [brackets]. Then list the facts you preserved.
Check: compare each statement with the notice. Do not add a room number, fee,
contact, promise, or new time.
Stop: do not browse, send, publish, or assume an unknown detail.
```

Keep the first request and first response. Mark each output statement:

| Check | What to look for |
|---|---|
| Source match | Can you point to the notice for every stated fact? |
| Shape | Did the response use two sentences and list preserved facts? |
| Unknown | Did it keep the room number as `[unknown]` rather than inventing one? |

If the response adds a room number, mark `FAIL` for that claim. Do not infer
that one response proves a model is always unreliable; this fixture shows only
one visible failure on one task.

For a second boundary check, write down what a text-only model can establish
about this sentence before you look anything up: **"The city library will
close at 6 p.m. today."** A model can discuss the wording, but the sentence's
current truth needs a source check. Do not ask it to invent a source.

## Four terms, only as far as this task needs

**Token.** A unit produced by a particular tokenizer and read or generated by
the model. It is often a word fragment, not a whole word. Token counts,
limits, prices, and speed are product- and tokenizer-specific.

**Context.** The information made available for one request: instructions,
conversation text, supplied material, retrieved passages, and tool results
where present. It remains a **finite context window**. Being inside it does not make a
passage true, and a model may fail to use an item correctly.

**Training versus this request.** Training shapes the parameters before use.
The current request supplies a new context; it is not a live lookup of every
fact in the world. A product can add current sources or tools, but that is a
separate evidence path. For a current claim, check the source and date rather
than relying on the cutoff alone.

**Sampling.** Generation can allow more or less variation depending on the
product's decoding controls. A lower variation setting is not a factuality
switch; a predictable answer can still be wrong.

Do not use parameter counts, a context-window number, or a temperature setting
as a substitute for checking the result on your task.

## What follows from the boundaries

LLMs are often useful for text-in/text-out work with a clear target: rewriting
supplied text, explaining a concept in another way, drafting an outline,
extracting fields, or proposing code that you can test. These are common
patterns, not guarantees.

They cannot, by themselves, establish that a citation is real, a website still
exists, a current claim is true, or a proposed action happened. A product may
provide search or retrieval, but returned material can be stale, incomplete, or
wrong. Before accepting it, **check the original source and date**.

The data boundary matters too. Before pasting, uploading, or enabling a
connection, check **what may leave the current surface and who authorized it**.
Do not turn a plausible draft into a payment, publication, deletion, or belief
without an explicit action boundary and evidence.

## The only completion check for this unit

Write a short explanation card without copying this page:

```text
My explanation:

Model boundary:
Product or tool boundary:
One reason a fluent answer can still be wrong:

Decision 1 (supported / not supported):
Evidence:
Decision 2 (model / product / tool):
Evidence:
```

Score your card against the unit rubric:

- `0` — it treats fluent text as proof, assigns agency to the model, or has no
  evidence for either decision;
- `1` — it mentions context-based generation but leaves a material boundary or
  verification reason unclear;
- `2` — it uses your own words, separates model from product or tool, and names
  one reason to verify on this task.

Keep the card, the first response, and one sentence about what remains
unknown. Do not report a course completion or learning result from this
self-check. The route's next operation is to turn an intention into a bounded
task card in [Context, instruction, and a first generation](../chapters/02-first-safe-task-EN.md).
