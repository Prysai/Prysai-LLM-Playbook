BEFORE YOU SEARCH

Write down your position before looking at properties.

A viewing is easier to assess when you already know which facts would change your decision.

A

Money available now

Record the deposit you can evidence, likely purchase costs, and a buffer. Keep estimates separate from confirmed figures.

B

Monthly limit

Decide what payment range you can sustain. A lender's maximum and your comfortable limit are not the same decision.

C

Timing and dependencies

List notice periods, tenancy dates, funds that need to clear, and anyone whose approval or work is required.

STOP RULE

If an important number is only a guess, mark it as a guess. Do not let a plausible page turn it into evidence.

03

QUESTIONS WORTH ASKING

Ask questions that can produce inspectable answers.

Replace “Is it a good home?” with questions about source, date, responsibility, and consequence.

PROPERTY

What is known about condition, alterations, boundaries, services, tenure, and recurring charges?

REQUEST: SOURCE + DATE
PROCESS

Which stage are we at, what remains provisional, and what event changes the legal or financial position?

REQUEST: OWNER + NEXT CHECKPOINT
COST

Which costs are confirmed, estimated, optional, recurring, or payable if the transaction does not complete?

REQUEST: AMOUNT + BASIS
CONFLICT

Who represents whom, who receives a fee, and which recommendation is independent?

REQUEST: ROLE + INCENTIVE
