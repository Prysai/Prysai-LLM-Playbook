# Typed rehearsal is not spoken conversation: an LLM practice boundary

**Status:** candidate research record; no learner session, model run, audio
sample, scoring exercise, or outcome evaluation was conducted.

**Accessed:** 2026-08-14 (America/Los_Angeles)
**Decision owner:** Prysai Lab curriculum maintainer
**Next review:** 2026-11-14, or before using this record to support a
reader-facing claim about spoken practice, model feedback, or assessment.

## Question and scope

**Question:** When a beginner uses a general, text-based LLM for a
second-language role-play, what may they call the resulting evidence without
mistaking typed success or model feedback for evidence of spoken conversation?

This record is limited to a text-only exchange: the learner reads a prompt,
types a reply, and receives generated text. It does not evaluate voice features,
speech recognition, pronunciation, a particular language, a named product, an
LLM as a tutor, or any learner outcome.

This is a deliberately distinct gap from the existing records on bounded
practice attempts and short prompt-count claims. It does not restate their
practice sequence, public user reports, or general no-fluency boundary. Its
single concern is the missing **modality evidence** when a typed role-play is
described as conversation practice.

## Source-bounded beginner implication

Call a text exchange what its retained evidence can show: **a typed rehearsal
of one specified language function under stated assistance**. The artifact can
contain the learner's typed reply, the model's candidate correction, and a
learner-authored revision. It contains no direct record of how the learner
heard speech, produced speech, handled timing, or repaired a live spoken
misunderstanding.

This is a project inference from O1-O3, not a learning finding. A small
beginner receipt can keep the boundary visible without adding another prompt
method:

| Receipt field | Record it as | Do not infer from it |
| --- | --- | --- |
| Mode | `typed rehearsal` | Spoken interaction, pronunciation, or listening ability |
| Target | One written function, such as asking for a train time | A general language level |
| Assistance | The model name/version if known, its feedback, and any outside reference checked | That the feedback was correct merely because it was fluent or confident |
| Artifact | Original typed reply and learner-authored revision | Independent oral performance |
| Unobserved | Speech, audio comprehension, pace, and live repair, unless separately captured | That absent evidence occurred successfully |

If the goal later becomes **spoken** conversation, change the evidence surface
before changing the claim: obtain an appropriately consented audio or live
interaction sample, specify the spoken target, and use a review method suitable
for that stated purpose. For any consequential placement, credential, or
instructional decision, a general LLM's feedback is not an assessment result;
the purpose and evidence need an appropriate qualified review path.

## Failure scenario

A beginner types a four-turn restaurant exchange in Italian. The model calls
the replies natural and says the learner is ready to converse. The learner
then treats that praise as proof they can understand a waiter speaking at a
normal pace.

The saved chat cannot support that conclusion: it contains typed production
with generated feedback, but no evidence of the learner hearing a spoken
question or producing an audible answer. Under O1, the correction itself may
also contain a confident error. The responsible receipt is therefore
`typed_rehearsal_observed; feedback_unverified_for_assessment`, with spoken
listening and speaking left `not_observed`.

## Non-claims

This record does not establish:

- that text role-play improves, harms, or fails to improve language learning;
- that a text exchange cannot be useful as rehearsal or preparation;
- that a learner who succeeds in text cannot converse aloud, or that one who
  struggles in text cannot converse aloud;
- that any particular model feedback, grammar correction, confidence statement,
  or generated rubric is wrong;
- that audio capture, a human reviewer, or a test score by itself proves
  fluency, retention, real-world communication, or a proficiency level; or
- that ETS assessment design or NIST guidance validates a Prysai card, a model,
  a language program, or a learner outcome.

## Next evidence

Before making any claim beyond the bounded receipt, an authorized evaluation
would need a declared beginner population and target language function; consent
and minimization rules for any text or audio; separate typed and spoken tasks;
predeclared aids and rubrics; retained original artifacts; reviewers suited to
the stated task; and a defined interpretation for each result. To examine
durability or transfer, it would additionally need later tasks and an explicit
claim boundary. None of that evidence exists for this record.

## Stop receipt

Research stopped after identifying primary owner material for generative-output
error risk, one language-assessment program's distinct skill surfaces, and
assessment-interpretation evidence. No public report was elevated into evidence
and no model, learner, voice feature, or assessment was tested. The resulting
curriculum implication is limited to labelling typed LLM conversation as typed
rehearsal and withholding spoken-conversation and feedback-validity claims
until matching evidence exists.
